The browser

Arc's best parts, on WebKit.

The parts of Arc people miss are here: spaces with their own animated themes, Favorites, Saved and Today, folders, tab groups, split view, a ⌘K command bar and auto-archive.

It runs on the WebKit already inside your Mac, so there is no second copy of Chromium to download, update and keep in memory.

  • ⌃NNew space
  • ⌃1Jump to a space
  • ⇧⌘DSplit view

    Quiet by default.

    No toolbar, no start page, no sidebar of suggestions, no account to sign into. The things people install extensions for are already here, each one keystroke away.

    • One field

      ⌘L

      Type an address and you go there; type words and you search. Nothing you type is sent anywhere until you press Return.

    • Tabs that stay out of the way

      ⇧⌘S

      Across the top or down the left. Pinned tabs shrink to a letter, and last session's tabs cost nothing until you click them.

    • Spaces

      ⌃N

      Rows of tabs with a name, an icon and a colour the whole column wears, and optionally a profile of their own.

    • Find any tab

      ⌘K

      Lists your open tabs by name as you type. The one you are on is always unmistakable.

    • Hide anything, for good

      ⇧⌘H

      Click a cookie banner or a newsletter overlay away. It stays gone on that site next time, before the page draws a frame.

    • Reading mode

      ⇧⌘R

      Strips a page down to the article.

    • An ad blocker that runs before the page

      Trackers and ad networks are stopped at the network level, so there is nothing to render and nothing to slow you down. On by default, off per site.

    • Video that follows you

      ⇧⌘P

      Lifts the video into a small window that stays above everything, including other apps.

    • Passwords, in your keychain

      ⌥⌘L

      Saved once a sign-in has actually worked, offered when you click the field, never filled on its own. Encrypted by macOS.

    • Updates itself, quietly

      A new build is downloaded and verified in the background first. Copper never restarts without your say-so. Light, dark, or the Mac's own — the frame and the pages follow.

    Moving in

    Moves in from Chrome or Arc.

    Flow is one button. It reads the other browser's files on your Mac and never changes them. Imported pages stay asleep until you visit them, and macOS asks once before handing over the other browser's key.

    Chrome extensions, without Chrome

    Paste a Chrome Web Store link, or press Add on the extension's page. It runs on WebKit's own extension engine, the one Safari uses, and Copper fills in the Chrome APIs WebKit lacks. macOS 15.4 or later.

    Built for agents

    We draw where your agents go.

    Copper is an MCP server. Claude Code, Codex or any MCP client can drive the browser you're already signed into. Copper draws where each agent is about to click and badges every tab with the agent working in it. Press Stop and the browser is yours again.

    • One switch. Settings › Agents › Let agents drive this window. Set up for Claude Code and phi is one press, no token pasted into a prompt.
    • A copper command for the shell, linked into your PATH by Homebrew. It never starts Copper on its own.
    • Loopback only, behind a bearer token readable by you alone. Pages in other browsers can't reach in.
    Jev, built in

    Ultrafast, and bring your own key.

    Hand Copper a goal, not a list of clicks. Jev reads the page's controls, picks an operation and an element in about 200 ms, and acts, instead of waiting on a large model for every click. A small model writes any text it has to type.

    Bring your own key: sign in with a Claude account, or point Copper at any OpenAI-compatible gateway.

    • Claude account
    • OpenAI-compatible gateway
    • Haiku, Sonnet or Opus

    • Four Jev tools on the MCP server

      jev_runjev_stepjev_observejev_extract

      Run a goal to the end, take one step at a time, see the page the way Jev sees it, or pull structured values out of it. The model's output never becomes a selector, a coordinate or JavaScript.

    • From your agent or your shell

      Type /jev <goal> in Claude Code or phi, or copper run "goal" in a terminal. The goal goes to Jev on the first call, with no tab list or snapshot first.

    • One click to set up

      Settings › Agents › Terminal agents: press Set up for phi or Claude Code, or Install for the CLI. Copper writes the config and the command for you, so no token is pasted into a prompt.

    • You can see who is driving

      A pill on the page says Jev is driving, and the driver timeline (⌥⌘J) shows every cycle: read, ask, act, settle. Press Stop and the browser is yours again before Jev's next action.

    One goal, start to finish.

    You are reading about copper on Wikipedia and want the article on the green that grows on old roofs. You hand Jev one sentence and watch the timeline.

    11.4 seconds, three actions. Jev clicked Search, a small model wrote the word, and Jev picked the article from Wikipedia's own suggestions. Then it stopped, because the article was on screen.

    1. CLICKSearch372 ms
    2. TYPE“patina” into Search Wikipedia290 ms + 1.3 s text
    3. CLICKPatina, change of object's surface…268 ms
    4. DONE, 96% sureverify it yourself

    DONE is Jev's claim, and the tool says so. Your agent is told to check the page before it tells you it worked.

    Sign-in for agents

    Agents log in, 2FA included.

    Connect Bitwarden and your agents use your saved logins, two-factor codes included, on your Mac or on a headless Mac mini. The password never reaches the agent, and you decide which logins they can use.

    • You choose what's shared. Logins in Bitwarden's Agents folder are shared; a copper-agent: deny field keeps one out for good. Agents can't change the rule.
    • Nothing secret comes back. A sign-in reports what was filled, never the password, the code or the session key.
    • Cards and addresses too, with browser_autofill. Driven by Bitwarden's own bw client, no extension.
    Canvas

    A multiplayer canvas in a tab.

    Canvas is a whiteboard that opens in an ordinary tab: stickies, text, frames, arrows, images and live websites. Your Personal canvas stays on this Mac and works with no network.

    Link Copper Cloud to share a board and you see everyone's cursors, agents included. The ⌘E agent, MCP agents and the copper command all read and write the same boards. Friends who aren't on Copper can join from any browser: the canvas is open source too.

    • ⌃⇧ENew canvas
    • ⇧⌘OPersonal canvas
    Canvas App

    The canvas is open source, in any browser.

    The board Copper shows in a tab is its own project, canvas-app. Copper ships its page, and Canvas App is the same page in Chrome, Firefox, Safari or Edge, with nothing to install. It keeps your boards in the browser, works offline, and joins shared boards live, cursors and all.

    MITEvery sticky, frame, arrow and live website on the board, in one repository you can read, build and fork.
    One page, everywhereCopper on the Mac and on Linux and Canvas App all run the same page, so a fix lands once.
    One file to hostThe whole app is one HTML file. Serve it from your own server, next to the canvases it syncs with.
    canvas-app on GitHub
    Copper Cloud

    Run your own Copper Cloud.

    Copper Cloud is the server half: one Rust binary and Postgres, installed with one command on any Linux machine, or with Terraform on AWS. It syncs spaces, tabs, bookmarks and history between your Macs and hosts live canvas rooms.

    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/copper-browser/copper-cloud/main/install.sh | sudo sh
    • Off until you turn it on, and it never carries passwords. You pick what syncs.
    • Pinned by its certificate. Copper shows the fingerprint before it sends anything.
    • Encrypted at rest with your account's key, on a machine you run.
    Privacy

    On your Mac, unless you say otherwise.

    The only things that leave your Mac are the pages you ask for, their icons, and one small request a day to see whether there is a newer version. Copper Cloud is the one exception, and it stays off until you link a server you run.

    No accountNothing to sign into with us. Copper Cloud accounts live on your own server.
    No telemetryNo analytics, and no crash reports sent anywhere.
    No server of oursYour tabs, history and passwords stay on the Mac, or on a machine you run.
    Where everything is
    WhatWhere it isWho can read it
    PasswordsThe macOS login keychain, as ordinary keychain items tagged Copper, or your Bitwarden vault if you connect oneCopper. Any other app triggers the system's permission dialog.
    History, bookmarks, open tabs, hidden elementsSmall JSON files in ~/Library/Application Support/Copper/You.
    Cookies and site dataWebKit's own store for the appThe sites that set them, as in any browser.
    ExtensionsUnpacked in Copper's own folder, their data in WebKit's extension storeEach extension, within the permissions you accepted.
    Copper Cloud, only if you link itThe parts you switched on, on the instance you linked, encrypted at rest with your account's keyYou, and that instance's operator.
    Anything elseNowhere.No one.

    A private tab (⇧⌘N) has its own cookie jar and leaves nothing behind when it closes.
