What's new in Copper.

Every build, newest first, in the words Copper's updater shows. Copper checks for a new build every six hours and updates itself.

Unreleased

The running list in CHANGELOG.md since 1.0, newest first. Most of it is already in the builds below; the newest lines may not be in a build yet.

  • Try Copper in the browser, moving from Arc, and every build's notes on the website. /try/ is a working copy of Copper's window (spaces with their colours, pins, split view with ⇧⌘D, ⌘K, tabs on top, hide anything, keyboard and Reduce Motion), and the homepage embeds it as try/?embed. /arc/ says what Move in brings from Arc today, what doesn't come, how to switch, and Arc's shutdown dates with The Browser Company's own pages as the source. /release-notes/ lists every build newest first, one anchor per version, written at deploy from each release's copper-version.json by .github/scripts/release_notes.py (notes about internal apps left out; --selftest checks it offline).
  • The website grows past one page. copper.exowatt.com gets a privacy page (what the app sends, where everything is, what the site knows), a press kit (facts, a ready description, the 1024 px icon, real screenshots, colours) and a real 404 page. Subpages share one stylesheet and template; the deploy writes the latest version into the page and a sitemap.xml, robots.txt points at it, and every page's assets, links, anchors, header and footer are checked before it ships. A daily rebuild picks up new releases. On CloudFront, /x/ serves its page and a missing one gets the 404 page (infra/site-aws.yml, applied by hand with admin SSO).
  • ⌘Y opens at once, however long your history. The History panel laid out every place you have ever been in one go — 20,000 after a move from Arc — and asked for every one of their icons, so ⌘Y hung until Copper was killed. Now the search runs off the main thread, the panel draws the newest 120 lines and the next 120 as you scroll to the end, and only the lines drawn ask for icons. Search takes several words in any order ("panel github"), and when no page has them all, the letters in order ("ghpull" finds github.com/…/pull/…). The count at the foot is of everything that matches.
  • copper shot takes a viewport size and a colour scheme. --size 390x844 lays the page out at that viewport in CSS pixels and --scheme dark (or light) makes it see prefers-color-scheme dark, for that one picture; the view is put back after, and both go with every other option (--full, --hd, --selector…). Headless, SEARCH_HEADLESS_SIZE now also sizes an agent's own tab, which stayed 1280×800 whatever it said.
  • A see-through window. The desktop now shows, blurred, through the sidebar and the margin round the page, with the space's colour laid thinly over it; the page itself stays solid. A space with your own picture or an animated scene keeps it as it was. Turn it off in Settings › General › See-through window; it also stays solid with Reduce transparency or Increase contrast on, and in full screen.
  • Tidy files your loose tabs into folders. With three or more loose tabs in a space, "Tidy" stands on the line above them (also ⌘K › Tidy Tabs): one click files them into a few named folders in Saved, reusing a folder the space already has when it fits, and Undo (or ⌘Z) on the pill that follows puts every tab back where it was. With a model set up in Settings › Intelligence, the model names the folders from the tabs' titles and sites only, never their pages; without one, or if it takes over 8 seconds, tabs are filed by site, two or more to a folder. A tab that fits nowhere stays loose.
  • A light on pins with something new. A pinned tab whose page title starts with an unread count, like (1) Paper, (3) Inbox or Gmail's Inbox (3), wears a small copper dot on its square in the sidebar and the tab strip until you go to it. A sleeping pin keeps its dot.
  • A morning page, once a day. Settings › General › Morning page takes an address; the first window, launch, return to Copper or wake after 3 AM opens it as the active tab (or brings back the tab already showing it), and every other window that day restores as it always did. Empty, the default, is off. The hour is daily.hour (defaults write com.collinrijock.copper daily.hour -int 4).

1.0.20261124.117 Latest

24 Nov 2026 · Release on GitHub

Sharing a tab can share its sound, and a second tab's microphone no longer silences your call. When a page asks for audio, the share card has Chrome's Also share tab audio switch: the stream then carries Copper's sound as well as the picture, so a meeting transcriber can hear the Meet tab. It is all of Copper's page sound, not only the chosen tab's, and the card says so; macOS asks once for permission to record it. And while one tab is on a call, another tab that asks for the microphone gets Copper's own instead of WebKit's, so the call keeps hearing you.

Copper now makes your new passwords. 1.0.20261007.42

7 Oct 2026 · Release on GitHub

Canvases have a checklist card: a title, Yes and No columns (or up to four of your own) and a row for each person or task, and one click on a box ticks it, so an RSVP or a to-do list takes seconds; older versions of Copper see the same list as a note with task boxes, and ticks show on both. Ticked task boxes in notes no longer cross the line out. Shared canvases have chat: the chat button beside Share (or C) opens it, typing @ picks someone on the canvas, and with Copper Cloud 0.6.0 they get a pill, a dot on the Canvas door and a Mac notification that opens the message. Settings is fast again: the rebuilt panel no longer lags when you scroll, hover, type or switch pages. 1Password works in Copper, natively: unlock with Touch ID through the 1Password app (or your account password, or a service account token), and your 1Password logins, codes, cards and addresses fill like Bitwarden's; new passwords can be saved to 1Password, and agents can sign in with items you share. Settings is rebuilt — larger, grouped and clearer — with a search that finds any setting as you type, typos and other names included. Canvas invites show where they stand. In a canvas's Share sheet, each person you invited reads Invited, with Resend to remind them and × to take the invite back. Invites waiting for you are listed under Invitations in the Canvas card, and the invite pill now shows over Settings and the other panels; its × means Not now, and the invite stays listed. An invite you already took up another way, such as through the canvas's share link, no longer sticks on screen. History sync no longer gets stuck on one oversized page. Downloads work from Gmail and other sites that fetch a file in a hidden frame, and pages no longer leave stray or empty files in Downloads.
