Moving from Arc

Keep your spaces. Bring them over.

The Browser Company is shutting Arc down on December 22, 2026. Copper is a free, open source Mac browser with spaces, pins and folders down the side, and it moves your Arc setup in with one sheet.

Download Copper Try the window first

macOS 14 or later · Apple Silicon or Intel · MIT licensed

What comes over

Move in reads Arc's files on your Mac and never changes them. Arc doesn't need to be open.

In ArcIn Copper
SpacesA space each, with its name, its emoji and its theme: up to three colours, the intensity and the grain.
Space profilesA space that used its own Arc profile keeps its own sign-ins in Copper too.
FavoritesPins at the top of the column.
Pinned tabsThe space's Saved tabs, in Arc's order.
FoldersFolders, nested ones and empty ones included, open or shut as you left them.
Today's tabsOpen tabs in the space. They don't load until you click them.
Split viewsSplit views of two pages. A third or fourth pane comes over as a tab beside them.
HistoryEvery page you visited, up to 20,000 places, for address suggestions.
Passwords, passkeys, sign-insInto your macOS keychain, and cookies so you stay signed in. macOS asks you once before Copper can use Arc's key.
Site dataWhat sites keep in the page, like settings and drafts.
ExtensionsChrome Web Store extensions install again in the background, on or off as they were. Needs macOS 15.4 or later.

Moving in again later adds only what is new. Undo takes back the tabs and spaces a move added.

What feels the same

  • The column

    Pins, the space's name, Saved and Today down the left, on the space's colour. The page sits beside it as a card.

  • Spaces that slide

    A swipe or ⌃1–⌃9 slides the column to the next space, as in Arc. Each one has its own colour and icon.

  • Split view

    ⇧⌘D puts two tabs side by side.

  • One box for everything

    ⌘T and ⌘K open one card for tabs, history and search.

  • Out of the way

    ⌘S hides the column. ⇧⌘S moves the tabs to the top if you'd rather.

  • A canvas in a tab

    Where Arc had Easels, Copper has Canvas: a whiteboard in a tab with notes, images and live websites.

What doesn't

  • WebKit, not Chromium. Copper runs on the engine inside macOS, the one Safari uses. Chrome extensions run on WebKit's extension engine, with the Chrome APIs it lacks filled in.
  • Next release: Peek (⇧-click), Little Copper (⌥⌘N) and Air Traffic Control link rules are built and ship in the next update. Boosts aren't built.
  • Not moved: Arc's archived tabs. Without Arc's keychain key, spaces still arrive, signed out.
  • Not notarized yet. Releases are signed with Copper's own certificate. Install says how to open it the first time.

How to switch

  1. Download Copper, or brew install --cask copper-browser/copper/copper. Keep Arc installed.
  2. In Copper's first window, pick Arc under Move in from. Later, it's ⌘K › Flow, or View › Move in from Another Browser… (⌥⇧⌘I).
  3. Leave the switches on and press Bring over. When macOS asks to use Arc's saved passwords, choose Allow.
  4. Make Copper your default browser, so links from Mail and Slack open there.

Arc's last weeks

The dates as The Browser Company gives them.

October 22, 2026No new Arc accounts.
November 23, 2026Arc Search on iOS and Android loses its AI features, sign-in and sync. Arc on Mac and Windows starts showing reminders.
December 15, 2026Last day to ask for a copy of your Arc account data, or its deletion.
December 22, 2026Arc stops working on Mac and Windows. Accounts are deactivated, and their personal data is deleted within 30 days.

Sources: A farewell to Arc on arc.net and Arc Sunset: Key Dates and What to Expect in the Arc Help Center, both by The Browser Company. Move in while Arc still opens, so you can check what came over against it.
