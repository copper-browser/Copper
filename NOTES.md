A Claude subscription is all Copper needs now. Sign in with your Claude account (Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise) in Settings › Intelligence, and the agent pane, Jev runs and the tools your coding agent calls all work. There's no API key or Jev key to find. Without a Jev key, Jev runs ask your Claude model for each step instead: slower than Jev, and nothing else to set up.

Copper always opens with a window. After an update, or a quit and relaunch, it could come up running with your tabs loaded and no window at all, and clicking it in the Dock, New Window and ⌘N did nothing. Now it opens its window even then, and every one of those brings it back.

Copper now makes your new passwords. On a sign-up or change-password form, click the new password box and choose **Suggest strong password**: Copper makes a strong one that keeps the site's rules, saves it to Bitwarden, 1Password or the keychain first — so a failed save never leaves you locked out — and fills it into the new and confirm boxes, with your current password in the old one. A password you type yourself on those forms is offered for saving to the right account, and agents can do the same with `copper autofill new-password` without ever seeing it.

With every window closed, clicking Copper in the Dock brings a window back — or opens a new one when none is left. It used to do nothing.

Copper is faster to open, to switch spaces and to come back to. Each space opens on the tab you left it on, and those pages load in the background after launch, so switching lands on a page that is already there; a tab you leave stays loaded unless memory is short or many pages are open, instead of reloading after half an hour; tabs brought back from your last session open from the cache; ⌘R checks with the server and reuses what hasn't changed instead of downloading everything again; a page loading no longer redraws the whole window; and a five-second freeze at launch with an animated space is gone.

Passkeys now keep the relying-party domain the site asks for, rather than substituting the sign-in subdomain. Copper's local authenticator reports internal transport only; it does not offer phone hand-off or iCloud passkey sync.

Mouse-look works: 3D pages and games that capture the mouse to turn their camera (first-person views, model viewers, anything on the Pointer Lock API) get it after you click in them, as in Safari and Chrome — Copper used to refuse every time, so moving the mouse turned nothing. Esc, or switching windows or apps, gives the pointer back.

Move in works from Chrome, Safari and Arc, and tells the truth. It is one steady sheet; when macOS keeps a browser's files private, its card says so and shows how to let Copper in, or how to use the browser's own export instead. The summary says what came over and what stayed, and moving in again adds only what is new. Settings had a usability pass: it works from the keyboard and with VoiceOver, fits a small window, asks before it clears or signs out, and its search finds more. Copper is now signed with its own certificate, so the macOS permissions you give it survive updates; give them once more after this update.

You can always see who is driving. The pill in the page's corner, the ring around it, the on-page trail and the timeline beside the page (⌥⌘J, now the Driver Timeline) that used to show only Jev runs now show every agent: phi or Claude Code calling browser tools over the loopback server, a bot through an agent link, the agent in Copper's own pane — one row per call in plain words, the latest reason the agent gave, and a Stop that takes the browser back (its next calls are refused, and it is told why). Bitwarden sign-in now takes email two-step codes and new-device verification codes: Copper runs bw login with its prompts on and asks you for the code as a second step, in Settings or through `copper bitwarden login`.

The first version. A browser for the Mac with nothing in the way: tabs in a row or down the side, pinned tabs that keep their place, and one field for addresses and searches. Ads are blocked before they load; passwords and passkeys stay in your keychain; anything on a page can be hidden; articles open in a reading mode and videos float. Chrome extensions install from the Chrome Web Store on macOS 15.4 or later — its button says Add to Copper. Tabs you haven't looked at for half an hour sleep and give their memory back. It runs on the engine already in macOS and weighs 2.9 MB.
